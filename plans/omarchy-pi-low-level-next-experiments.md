## Decision

Do not ship a tuning or source fix from this evidence. Select two concrete investigations: (1) THP allocation/retention under real browser pressure, with CMA/zram accounting to distinguish causes; (2) encrypted small-I/O scheduling overhead, with a baseline attribution gate before any workqueue change. Both are hypotheses with explicit rejection criteria. Governor and NEON-XTS candidates remain rejected. No security/feature reduction, background daemon, kernel recipe or shared shell/metrics edit is proposed.

The tracked v2 samples are different package/session states, not an A/B pair. In sample `348374ed`, AnonHugePages is 792576 KiB, CmaFree 2332 of 65536 KiB, MemAvailable 2215536 KiB, and zram `orig_data_size/compr_data_size/mem_used_total` is 49152/1820/163840 bytes. In `39e0f5af`, those figures are 825344 KiB, 25588/65536 KiB, 2040128 KiB, and 1343488/182982/1298432 bytes. Substantial anonymous THP and varying free CMA justify attribution, but neither sample demonstrates severe global pressure, a CMA failure or meaningful zram compressor cost. The six old metrics rows remain excluded. No performance percentage or energy saving is claimed.

The inspected base is `build/upstream-pi/linux-7.3.tar.xz`, with the primary audit's recorded archive SHA-256 `87363c24f10e06321eb362765521094e94471f6bff8bce2d6b3e40f39f004953` and recorded upstream commit `c03c1a14507dfaf8b856c2608f9ac216021fc222`. Per-file freshly computed SHA-256 values and numbered excerpts are in [source evidence](../tools/pi-experiments/low-level-source-evidence.json). The archive identity was inherited from the primary audit rather than recomputed here. Saved resolved config and recipe are the `kernel-reviewed/packages/resolved.config` and `kernel-reviewed/recipe/PKGBUILD` used for 7.3-32.1.

Important qualification to the earlier audit: the saved recipe **does modify mm/page_alloc.c** via `8205-mm-hint-uses-allocation-order.patch`. Read the actual patch: it passes original allocation order and an opportunistic-compaction classification through reclaim/kswapd, rather than confusing an order promoted by defrag_mode with the caller's order. It does not change the `alloc_flags_cma()` rule cited below. Do not describe allocator/reclaim in this installed candidate as pristine mainline. Also read ordered patches 8204 (opportunistic hints to shrinkers), 8206 (atomic paired order/hint) and 8207 (classify GFP_TRANSHUGE_LIGHT as failable); these affect interpretation of THP reclaim even without editing huge_memory.c. Hashes and affected paths are in the evidence JSON. The evidence JSON inventories patch overlaps for the inspected files; it is not a whole prepared-tree reproduction or a proof of every transitive dependency. No patch/build was run.

- [THP source](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/c03c1a14507dfaf8b856c2608f9ac216021fc222/mm/huge_memory.c), lines 61–70 and 1515–1546: `CONFIG_TRANSPARENT_HUGEPAGE_ALWAYS` initializes eligibility, while defrag initializes to `madvise`; ordinary non-madvised faults need not synchronously compact. Capture runtime `defrag`, per-size enabled modes, khugepaged settings and application `VmFlags`, not only top-level `enabled`. Switching to madvise does not split existing hugepages or prohibit an application's explicit hugepage requests. [Kernel THP documentation](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/mm/transhuge.html) describes the allocation/TLB tradeoff and multi-size policy. Modern MADV_COLLAPSE behavior also prevents interpreting madvise as an absolute prohibition.
- [CMA allocation rule](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/c03c1a14507dfaf8b856c2608f9ac216021fc222/mm/page_alloc.c), lines 3785–3792, allows movable allocations to use CMA; [CMA implementation](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/c03c1a14507dfaf8b856c2608f9ac216021fc222/mm/cma.c) manages contiguous allocations. `CmaTotal-CmaFree` is not a GPU attribution counter or proof that all occupied pages are pinned. Allocation success/failure counters and requester context are needed; do not enlarge CMA from one low-free sample.
- [VC4 driver selection](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/c03c1a14507dfaf8b856c2608f9ac216021fc222/drivers/gpu/drm/vc4/vc4_drv.c), lines 78–88, 237, 331–339 and 467: BCM2711 selects VC5, uses the GEM DMA dumb-buffer path, and initializes the legacy VC4 BO cache only for GEN_4. [V3D BO source](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/c03c1a14507dfaf8b856c2608f9ac216021fc222/drivers/gpu/drm/v3d/v3d_bo.c), lines 8 and 111–169: V3D uses shmem-backed scatter/gather pages and GPU mappings. Do not conflate scanout/DMA allocations, renderer BOs and the legacy VC4 cache. These are read-only attribution findings for the GPU owner, not proposed driver work here.
- [dm-crypt source](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/c03c1a14507dfaf8b856c2608f9ac216021fc222/drivers/md/dm-crypt.c), lines 2232–2255: bypass flags run conversion inline except hard IRQ/disabled-IRQ completions still queue on `system_bh_wq`. Lines 3364–3388: default crypto workqueue is unbound; `same_cpu_crypt` selects a per-CPU queue. Lines 1967–1970: no-write-workqueue also changes post-encryption write submission. These options are distinct experiments, not an all-flags recipe. [Kernel dm-crypt documentation](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/device-mapper/dm-crypt.html) documents these switches; high-priority explicitly trades away general responsiveness and is excluded.
- [AES registration](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/c03c1a14507dfaf8b856c2608f9ac216021fc222/crypto/aes.c), lines 576–605 and 707–720: `xts-aes-lib` is a wrapper calling library XTS with a linear-lowmem fast path and SG fallback. Priority 110 and its name do not prove scalar execution. Tracing farther resolves the implementation for this source/config: CONFIG_CRYPTO_LIB_AES_ARCH=y; lib/crypto/arm64/aes.h defines single-block AES hooks but no XTS arch override. lib/crypto/aes.c lines 1241–1317 therefore falls back to block-by-block XTS; single-block hooks use CE only with have_aes and may_use_simd, otherwise __aes_arm64_encrypt/decrypt. ASIMD alone does not redirect this XTS path to the separately registered NEON-XTS module. This establishes the compiled source path, not the transform held by the running root mapping or a reason to rerun the already negative module experiment. Library/arm64 dispatch excerpts are included in the evidence. AF_ALG in-memory throughput cannot measure dm workqueue hops or identify an existing mapping's transform. No rerun of the rejected NEON module trial is justified here.
- [UAS](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/c03c1a14507dfaf8b856c2608f9ac216021fc222/drivers/usb/storage/uas.c) submits kernel URBs; the `usbfs_memory_mb` accounting is in [devio.c](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/c03c1a14507dfaf8b856c2608f9ac216021fc222/drivers/usb/core/devio.c), the userspace USB device interface. Raising that limit is not a UAS block-I/O optimization. Actual active transport, bridge limits and reset/error history remain unknown in this lane.
- [Zram write path](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/c03c1a14507dfaf8b856c2608f9ac216021fc222/drivers/block/zram/zram_drv.c), around line 2304, invokes compression into RAM, without a root dm-crypt hop. [Zram documentation](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/blockdev/zram.html) distinguishes logical capacity, actual allocator usage and optional writeback. Zstd versus LZ4 is deferred until representative pressure demonstrates significant compression CPU time. Keep logical size/swappiness fixed if that later experiment earns a trial; never swapoff populated zram under pressure.

## Experiment 1: THP eligibility under representative pressure

Hypothesis: incidental hugepage allocation/retention increases browser working set or latency on this 4GB session. The candidate is runtime top-level `madvise` versus current `always`, with all defrag/per-size/zram/CMA/swappiness policies held fixed. It is not a kernel-config proposal.

Owner preconditions: exclusive trial window, existing versioned provenance, boot ID/monotonic timing, same package/browser builds, same physical output mode and ordinary idle policy, no competing compiler/media/Bluetooth/monitor activity. Save the complete current THP hierarchy and selected bracketed values. If top-level mode is no longer always, or explicit per-size modes defeat the intended comparison, report that and redesign rather than silently changing extra controls. Require a matched temperature window and zero native voltage alarm; stop at 75°C, a voltage alarm, new OOM/error or broken UI. Firmware clocks/energy remain unknown absent instrumentation.

Prepare one immutable, hashed offline browser corpus from the owner's real daily documentation/PDF/web-app session: twelve fixed tabs in a recorded order, all assets local, networking disabled during replay, a fresh copy of the same profile for each trial, extensions and browser flags identical. Record corpus/profile hashes, browser version and the exact automation steps. Without those artifacts and successful replay there is no representative experiment; do not substitute an allocation stressor and label it desktop performance. Script ten foreground tab switches plus scrolling each tab by the same fixed distance and back, then revisit the first tab. Use browser tracing for action-to-present latency, not a sleep or screenshot duration.

Run three blocks of A1/B1/B2/A2, 120 seconds maximum active replay per trial. Write only the saved top-level `enabled` control to select A=always or B=madvise; install a restoration trap before that first write and verify the original hierarchy afterward. Start a fresh browser/profile copy for every trial, because changing eligibility does not remove existing hugepages. Existing shell allocations remain a controlled constant; do not interpret this as full-session boot-policy qualification. Do not drop caches or force compaction. If replay never creates meaningful pressure, record that negative result instead of growing a synthetic balloon.

Use `low-level-snapshot.py --label thp-A1-before --pid <shell> --pid <browser-main> --pid <compositor>` and capture after each replay with matching labels; include all browser renderer PIDs discovered by the owner's process inventory, since main-process RSS is insufficient. The tool is one-shot and creates no child processes. Compare deltas of memory PSI `total` per elapsed monotonic interval, `compact_stall/success/fail`, `allocstall*`, `thp_fault*/collapse*/split*`, `pswpin/out`, pgscan/steal, CMA counters when exposed and zram physical bytes. Capture task-clock/context-switches with a separate bounded perf stat if available; keep tracing overhead identical across A/B. Read errors remain unknown. This snapshot does not replace existing versioned provenance or browser tracing.

Adopt only as a candidate for a later full-session test if all three blocks agree on at least 10% lower p95 action latency or at least 64 MiB lower aggregate browser PSS, with no more than 5% regression in the other primary metric or replay time, no OOM/new allocation failure, and no increase exceeding 5% in memory-full PSI time. Predeclare the primary metric; retain every trial, including failures. More THP or fewer compactions alone is not a user-visible win. If explicit app hugepage advice dominates, pressure is absent, results disagree or latency is below measurement resolution, retain the baseline and close this branch without a default change. These thresholds are proposed decision rules, not achieved results.

Zram compressor trial becomes eligible only if the same real replay uses at least 256 MiB original zram payload and profiling assigns at least 10% of CPU time to compression/decompression. A later zstd/LZ4 comparison needs separate empty-device/boot transitions owned by root, identical logical capacity/swappiness and matched fresh sessions. That is not part of the THP A/B. CMA sizing becomes eligible only with actual failed/slow contiguous allocations correlated to the workload and identification of its allocator/client; low CmaFree alone fails the gate.

## Experiment 2: encrypted storage scheduling attribution

Hypothesis: at queue depth one, scheduling/conversion handoff cost materially affects small reads even though a software-XTS transform change did not help. Do baseline attribution first. Do not benchmark raw root/SSD devices, dump `dmsetup table --showkeys`, expose keys or change cipher, key/sector size, integrity, barriers, discard, scheduler, autosuspend or mitigations.

Owner supplies a 256 MiB, fully allocated, non-sparse, incompressible regular file on the actual encrypted Btrfs mount, created once outside timing and checked by SHA-256; do not use zeros, a loop-backed encrypted filesystem nested inside root or tmpfs. Record Btrfs compression/mount/extent flags, filesystem free space, active mapping's non-secret cryptsetup status/queue flags, underlying block topology and active UAS versus usb-storage binding. Existing `allow-discards` remains unchanged. A missing `fio`/perf prerequisite is an experiment dependency, not authority to install packages in this lane.

Use the provided [read-only fio job](../tools/pi-experiments/low-level-storage-read.fio), invoking `--section=small-read` and `--section=stream-read` separately as its comments specify, with a distinct JSON output for every invocation. It refuses creation, uses direct I/O, one sync worker and depth one, 4 KiB random versus 64 KiB sequential reads, twenty seconds per run. Direct-I/O errors invalidate the run; never fall back silently to buffered/cache hits. Run three alternating small/stream pairs. Read-only fio and path review are both required; no unencrypted comparison file is needed. All setup/file creation is future owner work, not executed here.

Take before/after snapshots including the shell/compositor and fio PIDs where available. In one separately labelled, equally bounded profiling repetition, collect system-wide task-clock/cycles/instructions/context switches and a 49 Hz callgraph plus scheduler/workqueue trace (only available tracepoints, bounded duration/output) covering the fio process, kcryptd and USB completion contexts. System-wide tracing has overhead and unrelated tasks; never compare a profiled run with an unprofiled run. Record lost samples and symbol availability. Queue-delay attribution requires correlated bio/work item identity and timestamps, not counts of `kworker` wakeups. Distinguish time in AES/XTS, Btrfs mapping/checksum/decompression, block waiting, UAS/USB completion and kcryptd scheduling. The existing AF_ALG result is only a rough compute reference, not a storage ceiling.

Advance to a no-read-workqueue candidate only if at least two of three traces identify crypto queue waits/handoffs as at least 15% of small-read critical-path latency, without transport errors or dominant media/crypto execution explaining it. Otherwise close the workqueue branch and send the trace to the actual dominant owner. Absence of usable scheduler/bio correlation leaves attribution unknown and does not pass the gate.

Only after that gate, root should prepare a separate reversible active-mapping trial using its exact installed cryptsetup version and the complete original non-secret active flag set, preserving every existing option. First verify refresh/restoration semantics from that version's source/manual: omitted options must not silently reset unrelated flags. Do not generate a blind root `refresh` command from this offline lane. A disposable encrypted volume on the same transport would give safer preliminary mechanism evidence if one already exists; an encrypted loop file on encrypted root would double the stack and is not a representative replacement. No spare volume is assumed or requested here.

The future comparison is A=original flags, B=only no-read-workqueue added, three ABBA blocks with the same fixture and jobs. Require at least 10% lower 4 KiB p95/p99 latency, agreement across blocks, less than 5% regression in sequential throughput, CPU time/byte and desktop action latency, unchanged fixture digest, zero new storage/USB errors and independently verified restoration. Reject if inline conversion extends completion context and hurts interaction. Do not bundle no-write-workqueue, submit-from-crypt-cpus or same-cpu-crypt: they change different paths, and no-write also changes write submission. Write/fsync workloads would need a separately scoped bounded file and durability acceptance before proposing any write-path tuning. Nothing here establishes such a candidate.
