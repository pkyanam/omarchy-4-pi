# Owner-visible service retirement and host reference drain

November 8, 2026. **NONDEPLOYABLE.** Separable patch 0006 builds on immutable 0001–0005. MMAL is unchanged. Final six-patch source/artifacts are `replayed-close-01/`; editable source is `close-source/`. `edit.py` is the retained initial draft generator, not an idempotent final exporter; the final patch includes the subsequent callback-context guard correction. This unit changes only opt-in VCHIQ ownership/removal and SMIO integration, not VCSM dma-buf generations, firmware FREE/RELEASED, readiness or outer raw-handle admission.

## Implemented contract

`vchiq_open_service_owned()` installs one caller-provided owner and takes a service kref under the existing constructor mutex before RCU publication. Its separate completions are initialized before publication. Existing add/open/close/remove callers retain their signatures and behavior through the original constructor wrapper; the new exports are opt-in GPL kernel APIs. There is no allocation after publication that could fail to acquire the observer. Failed OPEN returns only after synchronous removal drains the service, including callback references. SMIO embeds owners in its instance, uses the owned open, and drains all opened owners on init unwind (including partial multi-service opens and failed worker creation).

`vchiq_remove_service_sync()` uses that pinned object, never a new lookup of an already retired handle. It marks closing (waking the real transport's quota/slot waiters), requests REMOVE through the existing slot handler poll mechanism, and waits uninterruptibly for the new broadcast retirement completion. The slot handler continues to own public-fourcc and service-state transitions; the caller does not invoke close internals under an invented lock. `vchiq_free_service_internal()` publishes retirement after FREE; this excludes new normal service lookups. An earlier interrupted legacy close may already have completed retirement, which the persistent completion records. There is no blind retry and no conversion of arbitrary EINVAL to success.

After retirement the owner releases its pin and waits for the final service kref's `service_release()` to complete a second broadcast completion, after table removal and userdata termination. The normal slot parser, sync handler, poll handler and bulk callers hold references around callback execution; final release therefore drains those references rather than merely observing a CLOSED callback. The RCU-delayed service allocation reclamation may happen later; it executes no client callback and needs no owner storage. No owner dereference follows completion publication. The new owner preserves object identity until the final drain, even after ordinary lookups reject FREE.

SMIO closes admission and wakes its worker, drains these owners with transport alive, joins the actual worker, then cancels remaining commands and releases its instance. The borrowed VCHIQ instance must outlive this sequence; SMIO never frees it. This removes the 0005 interrupted-close retry ambiguity and unsafe init close-error unwind. Successful host stop no longer relies on an interruptible close return.

The API requires one fresh owner per service, serialized open/remove, owner storage and transport kept alive through removal, process context outside all transport callbacks, and external exclusion of new raw SMIO acquisitions. Calling it from a callback can deadlock; the slot-handler warning is diagnostic, not a successful early return. A wedged firmware/handler or a reference that never drains blocks removal indefinitely. This is intentional retention of active ownership, with no timeout that frees callback userdata. Recovery/cancellation of a permanently stuck transport remains a larger contract and is not represented as ready here.

## Acceptance limits and next contract

`vchiq_shutdown_internal()` still discards `vchiq_remove_service()` errors; that function waits interruptibly. `vchiq_shutdown()` still frees bulk waiters and the instance and returns its initial zero status. Neither establishes general host quiescence for legacy services. This patch deliberately does not change that existing ABI/behavior for every caller. For the new owned service, finish remove_sync AND join SMIO before shutdown; concurrent shutdown or destroying owner storage while it waits violates the contract. Global shutdown modernization needs its own all-client/reference audit and must not wait under a mutex needed by draining clients. Failed connect before any service has different cleanup needs; production VCSM failed-connect transport balancing remains outstanding.

**Exact remaining OPENING progress gap:** in the actual `vchiq_close_service_internal()` OPENING branch, a locally requested removal queues CLOSE without advancing the state; a received CLOSE then sets CLOSEWAIT and completes the legacy remove_event, but does not signal the new retired completion or schedule another REMOVE poll. An OPENACK arriving after the first REMOVE poll similarly changes OPENING to OPEN without scheduling removal again. Consequently an owned failed/interrupted OPEN can wait indefinitely even with a responsive peer. The new owner pin safely prevents callback-userdata free in that case, but the init-unwind progress contract is incomplete. The test boundary deliberately completes removal directly after an injected OPEN-exchange failure, so those passing tests establish drain/ownership ordering only, not this real protocol progression. A follow-up must retain a synchronized owner removal intent across OPENING, requeue REMOVE on both rejection/CLOSEWAIT and late OPENACK (including the sync handler), and test the actual parser/close-internal bodies and service-use balancing. Do not replace this with retries, timeout/free, or assume injected removal proves the handshake. This is a coherent separable lifetime primitive, with this exact larger protocol-state change left open rather than claiming failed-open readiness.

The outer VCSM owner still must exclude raw-handle admission and own immutable per-generation state, device/DMA mappings and module code for old dma-bufs and asynchronous firmware FREE transactions. Current globals/devm/IDR teardown and later-generation routing are still unsafe. Firmware RELEASED, or an explicit firmware reset/drain guarantee, is required before releasing DMA mappings. Final host service references and worker join prove neither firmware DMA cessation nor successful RELEASED processing. No full VCSM failed-connect/remove/reprobe/outstanding-dma-buf acceptance is claimed. No Pi/SSH/GPU/benchmarks/installs/shared recipe edits/staging/commits/pushes/PRs/external messages including Kai/subagents were performed.
